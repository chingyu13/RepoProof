P5 [List Paragraph]: Purpose of Faculty of Engineering Umbrella Ethics
P6 [Normal]: Umbrella Ethics coverage for educational research carried out by CARE in the Faculty of Engineering was first obtained in [insert date after approval] from the University of Sydney Human Research Ethics Committee (HREC) to encourage education research amongst Faculty of Engineering academics and affiliates and to provide them with the appropriate ethics coverage for publishable research for conference presentations and publications.
P7 [List Paragraph]: Umbrella Ethics are for projects that use the same or similar data and/or are for the same/similar purposes, and/or involve the researchers from similar fields. So doing prevents the need for separate ethics applications and reduces the administrative workload of HREC and CARE researchers. Our common purpose is to evaluate and report on innovations explored by CARE.

P11 [List Paragraph]: Umbrella Ethics for retrospective education research
P12 [Normal]: The approval is for education research studies being conducted on retrospectively collected data from students involved in educational programs delivered by the Faculty of Engineering. Three main sources of data approved for research are: 1) routinely collected student-generated data from existing teaching and learning systems 2) evaluation data from student surveys and 3) curriculum documents for the operational delivery of teaching and learning. All data from the retrospective, routinely collected sources have been approved with a waiver of consent.
P13 [List Paragraph]: Umbrella Ethics - Prospective education research studies
P14 [Normal]: The approval is for all prospectively collected data from students as participants involved in educational programs delivered by the Faculty of Engineering.  Three main sources of data for the research are approved: 1) routinely collected student-generated data from existing teaching and learning systems to be utilised with a waiver of consent, 2) data from quantitative research methodologies (questionnaires or surveys) to be utilised with an opt-out consent and 3) data from qualitative research methodologies (interviews or focus groups) to be utilised with an opt-in consent process.
P16 [List Paragraph]:  Conditions you must meet to be covered by the Umbrella Ethics 
P18 [List Paragraph]: Reference the ethics name and number in any documentation
P19 [List Paragraph]: Be familiar with the Umbrella Ethics application, including data sources that are approved
P20 [List Paragraph]: Only studies that are minimal or low-risk will be approved (National Statement 2.1) and where the research benefits outweighs the risk. 
P21 [List Paragraph]: Studies must demonstrate that the research does NOT cause:
P22 [List Paragraph]: physical harm: including injury, illness, pain or death; 
P23 [List Paragraph]: psychological harm: including feelings of worthlessness, distress, guilt, anger, fear or anxiety related, for example, to disclosure of sensitive information, an experience of re-traumatisation, or learning about a genetic possibility of developing an untreatable disease; 
P24 [List Paragraph]: devaluation of personal worth: including being humiliated, manipulated or in other ways treated disrespectfully or unjustly; 
P25 [List Paragraph]: cultural harm: including misunderstanding, misrepresenting or misappropriating cultural beliefs, customs or practices; 
P26 [List Paragraph]: social harm: including damage to social networks or relationships with others, discrimination in access to benefits, services, employment or insurance, social stigmatisation, and unauthorised disclosure of personal information; 
P27 [List Paragraph]: economic harm: including the imposition of direct or indirect costs on participants; 
P28 [List Paragraph]: legal harm: including discovery and prosecution of criminal conduct 
P29 [List Paragraph]: All data will be initially downloaded with identifiable variables (e.g. student name, SID, email address). A CARE staff member that is independent of the research team is responsible for deidentification and maintaining a re-identifiable participant list. The independent staff member must give all participants a unique study code to protect participant privacy and maintain a separate coding document in the Research Data Store or OneDrive server. 
P30 [List Paragraph]: Only de-identified data s allowed during data analysis of the research project

P32 [List Paragraph]: Only applicable for studies wanting to use data from Units of Study that have been completed AND where all final marks for participants have been returned to those participants. 
P33 [List Paragraph]: For prospective collection of research data using quantitative and qualitative methodologies – 
P34 [List Paragraph]: Provide a Participant Information Statement using the proforma provided by the HREC
P35 [List Paragraph]: Obtain consent using the proforma provided by the HREC – preferably online or clarify that completion of the survey will indicate consent

P38 [List Paragraph]: De-identify data transcripts and quantitative data collections
P39 [List Paragraph]: Do not report closed or open-ended responses that would allow individual identification
P40 [List Paragraph]: Follow the University Research Data Management Policy 2014 and Procedures 2015, taking note that:
P41 [List Paragraph]: Qualtrics or RedCAP are the University-supported survey instruments that are preferred
P42 [List Paragraph]: OneDrive and Research Data Store are the preferred storage system
P43 [List Paragraph]: All data will be stored for 5 years.

P45 [List Paragraph]: Comply with the following HREC conditions:
